AI that works for your team, not the other way around.

I'm Ken Hopkins, a former nonprofit executive director. I help Canadian nonprofits build working AI workflows, then measure the hours and dollars saved.

Your staff are already using AI. There just isn't a plan.

Someone on your team is drafting emails in ChatGPT. Someone else is summarizing meeting notes in Copilot. A few people are quietly worried they shouldn't be.

Meanwhile, your team is stretched. The grant report is due, the board package is late, and nobody has time to figure out AI properly.

You are not behind. Only about 10% of Canadian nonprofits have a formal AI policy (Imagine Canada, 2026).

What changes when AI is part of how you work

Picture one recurring task that eats a few days every month. It might be a funder report, program intake notes or donor acknowledgements.

Together we build a workflow that produces a solid first draft. Staff review it and send it. We agree on what staff can and can't put into AI tools. Then we count the hours saved.

That is the whole idea. Small, working, measured.

All prices in Canadian dollars.

Why I do this

From 2013 to 2019 I was Executive Director of the School Lunch Association. We grew from about $1M to $3.1M in revenue. We reached 34 schools and about 950,000 meals a year, with an 85-person team.

Since then I have worked in senior university administration: budgets, workforce planning and employee relations.

I know what it is like to run lean. I also know that tools don't fix organizations. People and processes do. AI helps when it fits the way your team actually works.

That is why I stay accountable for making it work, not just setting it up.
