About Ken

I led a nonprofit through seven years of growth. Now I help nonprofits and social enterprises make AI work.

I'm Ken Hopkins, and I have spent my career in operations, first as executive director of a growing Newfoundland and Labrador nonprofit from 2013 to 2019, and since 2019 at a Canadian university. Draftera brings that experience to a single question. How can AI take routine work off the team at a nonprofit or social enterprise without adding risk or confusion?

01

2013–2019

Leading the School Lunch Association

From 2013 to 2019 I was Executive Director of the School Lunch Association in Newfoundland and Labrador. Over those years we tripled revenue and expanded to 34 school locations, serving about 950,000 meals a year with a team of 85 people.

Growth on that scale in a nonprofit rarely comes with matching resources. It meant hiring and scheduling across dozens of sites, reporting to funders and a board, and keeping quality steady through that growth.

The team consistently gave more than anyone asked of them, and the experience taught me that good systems are what protect good people.

2019 to present

Working at a Canadian university

Since 2019 I have worked at a Canadian university, where my focus is planning, strategy, oversight and implementation, along with employee relations and organizational change.

It is the practical side of keeping a large organization running, and it has given me a close view of how change lands with the people it affects, particularly when resources are tight and workloads are already full.

Draftera is my independent practice and is separate from my university role.

03

Why Draftera

Why AI, and why nonprofits and social enterprises

I build working AI workflows myself, and I have seen how much routine drafting, summarizing and reporting they can take off a team's plate.

I have also seen the gap that opens when staff experiment with AI on their own, without a plan or guardrails, while leaders are too stretched to step back and decide how it should fit. Nonprofits and social enterprises feel that gap most, because they have the least slack to absorb mistakes and the most to gain from time returned to their mission.

I started Draftera to close that gap for nonprofits, charities, social enterprises and associations, bringing an operator's perspective rather than a vendor's and measuring the results in hours and dollars.

04

How I work

The principles behind every engagement

These come directly from running organizations, not from the technology, and they shape how I approach every piece of work.

  • Start with one real task, a piece of recurring work that costs your team real time, so the value is visible before anyone commits to more.

  • Keep people in charge, with staff reviewing, correcting and sending everything a workflow produces, just as they do today.

  • Agree the guardrails early, in plain language, so everyone knows what can go into AI tools, what stays out and who checks the output.

  • Use the tools you already have, wherever possible. I am tool-neutral and have no product to sell.

  • Measure what changes, in hours and dollars, in a form you can share with your board.

  • Stay accountable, rather than handing over a tool and moving on. The work is finished when it is working for your team.
