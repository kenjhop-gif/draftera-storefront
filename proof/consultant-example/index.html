Illustrative scenario / not a client case study

What it looks like when a consultant’s method becomes a product.

Meet Harbourline Operations, a fictional but realistic boutique consultancy. This walkthrough shows how Draftera, a consulting partner, and an end client could work together from first conversation to follow-on engagement.

A note on the story

Names, companies, and events are illustrative. The roles, materials, and delivery flow reflect the work Draftera is designed to support.

The starting point

A good service, trapped in the founder’s head.

Harbourline Operations helps growing distribution businesses improve handoffs between sales, operations, and customer service. The work is valuable, but every engagement starts from a blank page and the founder carries discovery, analysis, and reporting alone.

Three parties

The consultant remains the trusted expert and client lead. Draftera makes the service easier to sell and operate. The end client receives a structured decision-making engagement, not a black-box assessment.

The engagement

From a sales conversation to a decision the client can act on.

In this example, Harbourline sells an Operational Bottleneck Diagnostic. The work is time-bound, evidence-led, and designed to create a confident next step rather than an open-ended analysis phase.

Week 0

The offer becomes clear

Harbourline positions a two-week Operational Bottleneck Diagnostic: a defined engagement that identifies the most consequential delivery friction and produces a prioritized action plan.

Week 1

Evidence is gathered

Cedar Peak completes a short intake, provides workflow documents, and introduces six stakeholders. Harbourline leads the important conversations; Draftera organizes source material and prepares candidate evidence.

Week 2

Patterns are tested

Stakeholders describe different final approvers. The workflow document has no named accountable owner. Customer-service staff report late notice of delivery changes. These are evidence points, not conclusions yet.

Review

Judgment is applied

Draftera prepares the evidence ledger, draft findings, and report structure. Harbourline reviews context, adjusts the prioritization, rejects weak claims, and approves the final client view.

Decision

The next engagement is earned

Cedar Peak receives a supported diagnosis and a 90-day roadmap. If the client chooses to proceed, Harbourline can sell the implementation work from a position of shared understanding.

A tangible deliverable

The finding is traceable. The recommendation is owned.

The diagnostic report does not ask the client to trust a score. It connects a practical recommendation to specific sources, then records the consultant’s professional review.

Operational Bottleneck Diagnostic / sample finding

Approval authority is inconsistent

Consultant approved
Source evidence

Three stakeholder interviews identify different people as the final approver. The documented workflow names no accountable owner.

Consultant interpretation

The workflow lacks clear ownership and decision rights. Delays are not isolated behaviour issues; they are a structural handoff problem.

Recommendation

Assign one accountable workflow owner, preserve specialist approval rights, and publish a single escalation path for exceptions.
